# Storytelling — Universal Philosophy (T0 canonical text)
<!-- scaffold: this is the single source of truth every skill points to. Benjamin rewrites the principles in his own language before anything else is drafted. -->

## Opening stance
- One paragraph: what storytelling is *for* — moving someone from what they believe now to what you need them to believe. Not information transfer.
- State the author's authority in one line (Airbnb inclusive design, Cash App Trust, executive storytelling) — the emil pattern: grounded in shipped work, not theory.

## Principles (each: name → one-line rule → the why → evidence tag)
- **Story is a change of belief** — why: narrative transportation (Green & Brock 2000) [R]; Duarte's "what is / what could be" [P]
- **Contrast is the engine** — why: McKee's gap, Olson's ABT, Duarte's sparkline; Kahneman loss/gain asymmetry [R]
- **A throughline outranks a collection of good ideas** — why: cognitive load (Sweller) [R]; Olson's AAA failure mode
- **Concrete beats abstract** — why: transportation, identifiable victim effect (Small/Loewenstein/Slovic 2007) [R], picture superiority [R]
- **Tension precedes resolution** — why: information-gap curiosity (Loewenstein 1994) [R]
- **Endings are weighted** — why: peak–end rule (Kahneman/Fredrickson) [R], recency [R]
- **Order is meaning** — why: primacy/recency [R]; Minto answer-first [P]
- **Claims need receipts** — why: Aristotle's logos; Minto's discipline; your own "locked figures" rule
- **The audience is the hero, you are the mentor** — why: Duarte's inversion of Campbell [P]
- **Compression is a feature** — why: curse of knowledge (Newton 1990) [R]; the short forced version finds the real story

## The five questions (asked in order before any drafting)
1. Who is this for, and what do they already believe?
2. What is the one thing they must believe by the end?
3. What tension makes that belief worth changing?
4. What proof makes the change credible?
5. What shape does this audience need it delivered in? (→ pick-story-structure)
