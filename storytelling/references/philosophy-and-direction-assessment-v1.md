Output: what goes into `references/philosophy.md`, what changes the overall direction, what changes the process, and what each of the 12 skills inherits.

## 1. The philosophy — what goes in `references/philosophy.md`

Twelve beliefs. Each is stated as you wrote it, then what it means for a designer. Marked ★ where the belief is distinctively yours rather than adopted; those are the ones that make the pack yours and not a Sinek-Duarte remix.

1. **Belief is the currency.** "Your work lives or dies on whether the room believes in it." Belief is not a by-product of craft.
2. **A pitch is a story with a goal.** Every review, readout, crit, talk and interview is a pitch. Stories communicate; pitches communicate to move someone.
3. **Two steps to belief.** Know what you believe (harder than it sounds); express it through a story. Skip step one and nothing else works.
4. **Inside-out.** Why → audience → goal → content → visuals → delivery. Adopted from Sinek, but the *ordering of the whole method* around it is yours.
5. ★ **Slides last, on purpose.** Everything is built before the deck exists so the deck can never become a crutch. For designers this is the entire book: the deck is not the work and not the portfolio.
6. ★ **Two realities.** Every pitch has a world before and a world after; the story carries the room from one to the other. This is also your slide brief (FROM left, TO right) — the philosophy and the visual system are the same idea.
7. ★ **You're hardwired to be bad at this.** Curse of knowledge; speaking to the neocortex when the croc brain is the gatekeeper; and for designers, a third — you were trained to let the work speak for itself.
8. ★ **Fear is energy.** Learned, therefore controllable; never goes away and shouldn't; "the goal is not to speak without anxiety, it is to positively influence your audience."
9. **Practise is the key to all of it.** Deliberate, out loud, in front of others. "Every summary should have the word practise in it."
10. **Lean applied to storytelling.** Pitch early, pitch often. Pitch → feedback → iterate → repeat. Minimum effective dose. Good enough beats perfect.
11. ★ **The audience, not you — and that is also the fear cure.** "We can't focus inwards and outwards at the same time." The deck is for them. The goal is what they need to hear.
12. ★ **You are already an actor.** You play lover, child, leader, colleague fluently; the presenter just defaults to The Timid Speaker. Designer version: The Narrator ("and then the user taps here") vs The Owner.

Plus one adopted rule worth keeping as a belief: **feedback is a gift, not a request for change** (Corey Ford).

Two things in the dossier's philosophy list I would *not* put in philosophy.md: "the best storyteller wins, not the best fiction" (it's a rule inside drafting, not a belief) and the Fearless manifesto/public-commitment device (course mechanic, not doctrine).

## 2. What changes the overall direction

The designer adaptation layer is the real news. Six things in it change the pack, not just individual skills.

**The Seven replaces the twelve investor headings, and need → vision → insight is its spine.** Need, cost, insight, vision, design, evidence, ask. This resolves an open question from the v1 scaffold: `need-vision-insight` is not a separate skill, it is the default plot every narrative skill assumes. Open with the user, not the screens.

**Four design stories.** User, Team, Craft, Business. These are the story *types* a designer chooses between — the throughline skill's first question is "which of the four is this?"

**Six rooms.** Crit, design review, exec readout, roadmap conversation, all-hands/conference talk, portfolio interview. The 20-minute exec review is the default. These become the modes of `adapting-for-the-audience` and the scenarios in every "Use it when".

**Executives as pattern-matchers.** Ten minutes and a budget; decide with the croc brain, rationalise with the neocortex. Four exec-voice questions that every slide must survive: "Is this a real problem or a design problem?", "What does it cost me to say yes?", "Why now?", "What happens if we do nothing?"

**"Your deck is gorgeous. That's why it failed."** The fear-of-deck diagnosis flipped for designers: too many screens, deck as portfolio, over-designed slides, the twelve-directions exploration slide. Five rules designers actually break: one idea per slide; the 3-second rule; before beside after, always; never a chart without its sentence; no exploration slides in a decision room.

**Prototypes over presentations.** Live Figma is credibility and where reviews die. Never present from the canvas. Never say "this is still rough." And the two live failures with a script for each: the room jumps to the screens; the room redesigns it live — name it, park it, return to the story.

## 3. What changes the process

**A section format for every skill, from the Fearless course.** Your own lesson structure was Secret → Supporting evidence → Action step. That is the shape each SKILL.md section should take, and it matches how you want things explained to you. Concretely: state the rule in one line → why it works (one citation or one story) → the drill or template.

**Agent-native reviewer modes.** The dossier lists six the content supports, and they are the most valuable thing an agent can do that a book cannot: the three-things simulation (agent plays the exec and reports what it remembered); the So-what pass over a script; an objection generator in an exec's voice; a deck fear-audit; a fluff scan; a Q&A rehearsal partner using the difficult-reviewer types. These belong in `rehearsing-and-taking-feedback` as its critic mode, and the fear-audit in the slides skill.

**Templates become the skill outputs.** Preparation statement, why statement, the Seven outline, interviewer brief, practise log, Wish/Question/Love/Hate sheet, storyboard boxes, slide audit, objection list, night-before checklist, one-page POV. Each skill should end by producing one of these, not prose advice.

**A voice reference.** The dossier's Section 6 is a usable `references/voice.md`: second person, present tense, rhetorical question then one-word answer, actor's-toolkit and lean-startup metaphors, a failure story before every method, British spelling, every section ends in an action.

## 4. What each of the 12 inherits

- `storytelling` — the twelve beliefs; the diagnostics list (why-check, passion-vs-metrics test, two-realities test, everyone test, so-what, three-things, cue-card, good-enough, bluff); voice rules.
- `storytelling-for-designers` — the designer adaptation layer in full: six rooms, four stories, the Seven, exec questions, designer fears, Narrator vs Owner, prototypes over presentations.
- `storytelling-for-engineers` / `-researchers` — nothing yet. The dossier is designer-only. These two lenses need their own adaptation layer written from scratch.
- `finding-the-throughline` — which of the four stories; the why statement; one goal via the focusing question; "do I need a pitch or a document?" (POV vs PRD); the audience persona with the four designer additions; the preparation statement.
- `drafting-the-narrative` — the Seven as default plot; brain dump → one thing per heading; the no-words interview (interviewer is a PM or engineer, never a designer); the so-what drill with the bottom-sheet example; opening and flow rules.
- `turning-narrative-bullets-into-slides` — every slide answers a question, in the exec's voice; the three kinds of content to pull; storyboard boxes; the fear-audit; demo-or-not with designer fallbacks.
- `choosing-the-right-slide-template-for-each-beat` — the Seven map to layouts; before-beside-after is the vision beat's template; chart-plus-sentence is the evidence beat's; under eight templates.
- `polishing-slides-with-motion-and-visual-craft` — the five rules designers break; kill your darlings at full weight; animation only to explain; no exploration slides.
- `adapting-for-the-audience` — the six rooms; the 20-minute exec review as default; "the case study is a pitch."
- `rehearsing-and-taking-feedback` — the loop; the table read; three things; the feedback sheet; feedback handling; strangers = outside your team, never other designers; the 20-hour plan by deadline; What-Ifs as the objection list drafted with a PM; the six agent critic modes.
- `delivering-on-the-day` — fear as energy, the Big 6 mapped to the crit, the three reframes (acknowledge it; it's a conversation, use questions; it's about them); breath drills; night-before checklist with designer additions (real room, real screen, guest wifi, offline prototype, PDF export, the ask on a card); the two live failures and the park-and-return script; Q&A rules; the difficult-reviewer types; one-page POV leave-behind.
