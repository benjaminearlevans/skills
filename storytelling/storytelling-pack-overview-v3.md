# Storytelling skills

A skill for every part of telling your story — from the jumbled mess of ideas in your head to the room going quiet when you finish.

Install them into your coding agents with a single command:

```
npx @dsgnedge/install --token=YOUR_TOKEN
```

Run it in your terminal, choose whether to install into the current project or globally, and pick the skills you want. You can rename skills during install, or grab specific ones directly, like `npx @dsgnedge/install finding-the-throughline`.

## Available skills 12

Grouped by the three Ds — Define, Design, Deliver — in the order you'd use them on a real story. Every skill works from the inside out: your why first, then your audience, then the content, and the slides last. Click any skill to see what it does.

## Define — crafting the narrative

Working out what you're saying, who it's for, and what has to be cut so the rest can land.

- **/finding-the-throughline** — Use it when you have a pile of material, five good points, and no single thread holding them together. Covers: is there a story here, who's the room and what's the goal (the 5 Ws, the preparation statement), and the one sentence everything hangs from.
- **/drafting-the-narrative** — Use it when you have a throughline and need it to become a story with stakes, not a list of facts. Covers: choosing the shape (arc, need-vision-insight, SCQA), the beat skeleton, the "so what?" test, and talking your way to a first draft when the page is blank.

---

## Design — making the story visible

Turning the narrative into slides that support you without stealing the show.

- **/turning-narrative-bullets-into-slides** — Use it when the story is written and you're about to open Keynote with no idea what each slide is for. Covers: storyboarding on paper, one idea per slide, writing each slide's message from the audience's point of view, and deciding what to show versus tell (including whether to demo).
- **/choosing-the-right-slide-template-for-each-beat** — Use it when every slide looks the same, or every slide looks different. Covers: matching beat types to a small set of layouts — the single idea, the FROM/TO contrast, the insight-not-data slide, the evidence slide, the section marker — and keeping the set under eight.
- **/polishing-slides-with-motion-and-visual-craft** — Use it when the deck works and you want the pass that makes it feel finished. Covers: hierarchy, type, colour, imagery, the 3-second rule, when animation earns its place (only to explain a concept), and killing every darling that doesn't.

---

## Deliver — moving the audience

Getting the story from your head into the room, and moving people from where they are to where they need to be.

- **/adapting-for-the-audience** — Use it when a story that landed with one room is falling flat in another. Covers: same skeleton reshaped for executives, peers, a panel, or Q&A.
- **/rehearsing-and-taking-feedback** — Use it when you've never said it out loud to anyone and the real audience is next week. Covers: deliberate practice self → friends → strangers, the wish/question/love/hate sheet, "what are the three things you remember," separating information from emotion, the What-Ifs list, the dry run, and knowing when it's good enough. Also runs as a strict critic against your draft or whole deck when you have no humans handy.
- **/delivering-on-the-day** — Use it when it's tomorrow and you want to know what to do tonight, this morning, in the ten minutes before, and when the questions start. Covers: calming the fear, the night-before checklist, warm-up, tech failure, handling questions, and follow-up.

## The foundations

Read or load these when you want the underlying philosophy rather than a specific job done.

- **/storytelling** — The core philosophy. What makes something a story, why you start with why, and the rules every other skill inherits. Includes the storytelling vocabulary as a reference.
- **/storytelling-for-designers** — The designer's lens: case studies, critique, portfolio work.
- **/storytelling-for-engineers** — The engineer's lens: technical narratives, RFCs, architecture decisions.
- **/storytelling-for-researchers** — The researcher's lens: findings, synthesis, insight decks.
