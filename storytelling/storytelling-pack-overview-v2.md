# Storytelling skills

A skill for every part of telling your story — from the jumbled mess of ideas in your head to the room going quiet when you finish.

Install them into your coding agents with a single command:

```
npx @dsgnedge/install --token=YOUR_TOKEN
```

Run it in your terminal, choose whether to install into the current project or globally, and pick the skills you want. You can rename skills during install, or grab specific ones directly, like `npx @dsgnedge/install narrowing-too-many-ideas-into-one`.

## Available skills 24

Grouped by the three Ds — Define, Design, Deliver — in the order you'd use them on a real story. Every skill works from the inside out: your why first, then your audience, then the content, and the slides last. Click any skill to see what it does.

## Start here

The three that cover most of what you'll do. One for each stage: working out what you're saying, making it visible, and getting it into the room.

- **/narrowing-too-many-ideas-into-one** — Use it when you have five good points and no single thread holding them together.
- **/turning-story-beats-into-slides** — Use it when the narrative is solid but the deck doesn't show it.
- **/rehearsing-until-they-hear-what-you-said** — Use it when you've never said it out loud to anyone and the real audience is next week.

## Define — crafting the narrative

Working out what you're actually saying, who it's for, and what has to be cut so the rest can land.

- **/finding-your-why** — Use it when you can explain what you built but not why anyone should care.
- **/spotting-a-story-in-your-material** — Use it when you have raw notes, research, or a finished project and can't tell whether there's a story in there at all.
- **/knowing-the-room-before-you-write** — Use it when you're already drafting and haven't pinned down the goal, the audience, or where and when you're presenting.
- **/narrowing-too-many-ideas-into-one** — Use it when you have five good points and no single thread holding them together.
- **/choosing-the-right-shape-for-your-story** — Use it when you know what you're saying but not what arc or structure it should take.
- **/turning-beats-into-a-narrative** — Use it when your material reads like a list of facts instead of a story with stakes.
- **/talking-your-way-to-a-first-draft** — Use it when you can talk about the work for an hour but freeze at a blank page.
- **/building-a-need-vision-insight-pitch** — Use it when you're pitching strategy and need it framed as need, vision, and insight rather than a project update.
- **/finding-the-right-storytelling-term** — Use it when you can describe what you mean but don't know what it's called, so you can't brief anyone on it.

---

## Design — making the story visible

Turning the narrative into the artefact — slides, visuals, a demo — and cutting everything that doesn't earn its place.

- **/storyboarding-before-you-open-keynote** — Use it when you're about to start designing slides and haven't decided what each one is for.
- **/turning-story-beats-into-slides** — Use it when the narrative is solid but the deck doesn't reflect it.
- **/deciding-whether-to-demo** — Use it when you're not sure whether to show the real thing live, screenshot it, or skip it.
- **/cutting-what-doesnt-earn-its-place** — Use it when the deck is finished and forty slides long.
- **/stress-testing-a-narrative-draft** — Use it when you've written it and want it torn apart before someone else does it for you.
- **/prioritizing-fixes-across-a-deck** — Use it when a whole deck or portfolio has problems and you don't know what to fix first.

---

## Deliver — moving the audience

Getting the story from your head into the room, and moving people from where they are to where they need to be.

- **/adapting-your-story-for-a-different-audience** — Use it when a story that landed with one room is falling flat in another.
- **/rehearsing-until-they-hear-what-you-said** — Use it when you've never said it out loud to anyone and the real audience is next week.
- **/taking-feedback-without-taking-it-personally** — Use it when someone's notes stung and you can't tell what's useful and what's noise.
- **/preparing-for-what-could-go-wrong** — Use it when you want a plan for the projector dying, the hostile question, or forgetting your lines.
- **/getting-through-the-day-of** — Use it when it's tomorrow and you want to know what to do tonight, this morning, and in the ten minutes before.
- **/handling-the-questions-after** — Use it when the talk is done and the Q&A is where it could still go wrong.
- **/calming-the-fear-before-you-speak** — Use it when your heart is racing and you need it to work for you, not against you.

## The foundations

Read or load these when you want the underlying philosophy rather than a specific job done.

- **/storytelling** — The core philosophy. What makes something a story, and the rules every other skill inherits.
- **/storytelling-for-designers** — The designer's lens on top of the core philosophy: case studies, critique, portfolio work.
- **/storytelling-for-engineers** — The engineer's lens: technical narratives, RFCs, architecture decisions.
- **/storytelling-for-researchers** — The researcher's lens: findings, synthesis, insight decks.
